Ball tracking for clubs, academies & coaches.

By the GullyDRS team · Updated 16 November 2026

Short answer: GullyDRS turns one phone fixed behind the bowler into a ball-tracking and video analysis tool for cricket nets. Clubs, academies, schools and coaches can review LBWs like the TV DRS and – in testing – get bowling analytics for every ball: speed, pitch map, beehive and line & length. Everything is processed on the device; nothing is uploaded.

Who it’s for

  • Cricket academies and coaching centres – give every bowler data from every net session.
  • Cricket clubs – settle LBWs in practice games and grassroots matches.
  • Schools and colleges – affordable ball tracking for PE and school teams.
  • Indoor, box and net cricket venues – a fixed camera in the same lane is the most accurate GullyDRS setup.
  • Independent coaches – show players where their deliveries actually pitch, not where they think they pitch.

LBW reviews for practice and club matches

Record the delivery and GullyDRS shows the ball’s path with the three DRS checks – pitching, impact and wickets – and a clear decision: out, not out or umpire’s call. If it can’t track the ball reliably, it says inconclusive and the on-field decision stands. When the ball clearly hits the bat before the pad, it gives not out. New to the details? Bowling analytics for coaches In testing

Using the same tracking engine as the DRS, GullyDRS can analyse a whole over or net session from one fixed camera, measured in real metres rather than pixels:

MeasureWhat coaches get
SpeedSpeed of every ball (km/h), with session average, fastest and spread
Pitch mapWhere every ball pitched, coloured by length and numbered
Line & lengthLength zones for pace or spin (yorker, full, good, short…) and line zones; “good area” percentage
BeehiveWhere every ball would have passed the stumps – and how many were hitting
MovementDeviation off the pitch (seam or spin) and approximate swing in the air
ConsistencyA consistency score from accuracy, grouping and speed spread
ReportsA shareable summary picture, a full offline report and a spreadsheet (CSV) for Excel

Record a whole over or session in one video and it is split into deliveries automatically. Balls the tracker can’t follow reliably are listed as not tracked and never used in the statistics – an honest gap beats a made-up number.

Bowling analytics is in testing; features and names may change before launch.

Setting up in your nets

  1. Mount a phone on a tripod behind the bowler, in line with the stumps.
  2. Mark the stumps – or the four crease corners – once. Every ball of the session reuses that setup. Marking the creases gives the best precision.
  3. Record the session – one clip per ball, or the whole over in one video.
  4. Review each LBW on the spot, or open the session report after practice.

Because the camera stays in the same place every week, a net lane is the most accurate place to use GullyDRS.

Safe for junior players

Academies coach children, so footage matters. GullyDRS processes video on the device: no account, no cloud upload, and we never see your videos. Your academy decides what is kept and what is shared. As always, get permission from a parent or guardian before filming under-18s. See our privacy policy.

How it compares with broadcast ball tracking

TV ball-tracking systems such as Hawk-Eye use several high-speed cameras and specialist operators. GullyDRS uses one phone. It is built for practice, coaching and grassroots decisions – not to replace the systems used in international cricket. In return it costs a fraction, sets up in minutes and works anywhere, even without Wi-Fi.

Frequently asked questions

Can cricket academies and clubs use GullyDRS?

Yes. GullyDRS is most accurate with a fixed camera in the same net, which makes it a good fit for academies, clubs, schools and coaches. It reviews LBWs and, in testing, adds bowling analytics for every ball.

Can GullyDRS measure bowling speed?

Yes – in testing, GullyDRS estimates the speed of every ball from one fixed phone camera and reports the session average, fastest ball and spread in km/h.

What equipment do we need?

One phone and a tripod behind the bowler, in line with the stumps. No extra cameras, sensors or laptop are required.

How accurate is it?

Accuracy is best with a fixed camera in the same net and the crease corners marked once. Balls the tracker can’t follow reliably are listed as not tracked and left out of the statistics, and LBW reviews say inconclusive rather than guess.

Does it work for spin bowling?

Yes. GullyDRS uses separate length zones for pace and spin and measures how much the ball deviates off the pitch.

Can we export the data?

Yes. Each session produces a shareable summary picture, a full offline report and a CSV file for Excel.

Is footage of junior players uploaded anywhere?

No. Videos are processed on the device and never uploaded. There is no account, and the academy controls what is kept and shared.

Is GullyDRS the same as Hawk-Eye?

No. Broadcast systems like Hawk-Eye use several high-speed cameras. GullyDRS uses one phone and is built for practice, coaching and grassroots cricket.
