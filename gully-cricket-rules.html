Gully cricket rules: the street edition.

By the GullyDRS team · Updated 16 November 2026

Short answer: Gully cricket (also called street cricket, and tape ball cricket in Pakistan) is informal cricket played in streets, lanes and open plots across Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka – the same spirit as backyard cricket in Australia, England and New Zealand. There is no official rulebook – every street has its own house rules. Agree them before the first ball.

What is gully cricket?

“Gully” means a lane or narrow street. Gully cricket is the cricket almost everyone in South Asia grows up with: a few friends, a bat, a tennis or tape ball, and whatever is nearby for stumps – bricks, a crate, a chair or chalk lines on a wall. Teams are small, boundaries are walls and houses, and the rules change from street to street.

What is tape ball cricket?

Tape ball cricket is played with a tennis ball wrapped in electrical tape. The tape makes the ball heavier, faster and lets it swing, while staying safe enough to play without pads. It is especially popular in Pakistan, where night tape ball tournaments draw big crowds – and it has produced several international fast bowlers.

Common gully cricket rules

These are the house rules you will hear most often. None of them is “official” – pick the ones your group likes and agree before you start.

  1. One tip, one hand (OTOH): a catch taken with one hand after one bounce is out.
  2. Over the wall or into a house is out – and the batter usually has to fetch the ball.
  3. Direct hits only: hitting certain walls or windows can be out, or score fixed runs (often 4 or 6 for a particular wall).
  4. No LBW – unless everyone agrees to play it. (If you do, use the real LBW rules.)
  5. Trial ball: the first ball of a new batter or bowler “doesn’t count”.
  6. Last man batting: the last batter can bat alone without a partner.
  7. Everyone bowls: overs are shared so every player gets a turn, with a limit per bowler.
  8. No running on the off side / one side only: on narrow streets, runs are often scored only from certain directions.
  9. Wides and no-balls: usually an extra run and a re-bowl – but many streets say “no wides on the leg side”.

How to settle the arguments

Every gully match has one: “That was going down leg!” “It hit the bat first!” A few things help:

  • Agree the house rules – especially LBW and catches – before the toss.
  • Let the non-batting player who is not bowling act as umpire.
  • Use a review: put a phone behind the bowler and record the delivery.

Frequently asked questions

What is gully cricket?

Gully cricket is informal street cricket played in lanes, streets and open plots across South Asia – much like backyard cricket elsewhere – usually with a tennis or tape ball and house rules agreed by the players.

What is tape ball cricket?

Tape ball cricket is played with a tennis ball wrapped in electrical tape, which makes it heavier and faster and helps it swing. It is especially popular in Pakistan.

What does one tip one hand mean in gully cricket?

One tip one hand (OTOH) is a common gully cricket rule: a catch taken with one hand after the ball bounces once is out.

Is LBW allowed in gully cricket?

Usually not – most streets play without LBW because there is no neutral umpire. If your group plays it, agree the rules before the match or use a review app like GullyDRS.

What is the pitch length in gully cricket?

There is no fixed length; it depends on the street. Official cricket uses 22 yards (20.12 metres) between the stumps.
