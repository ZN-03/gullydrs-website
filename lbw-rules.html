LBW rules in cricket, explained.

By the GullyDRS team · Updated 16 November 2026

Short answer: LBW means Leg Before Wicket (Law 36 of the MCC Laws of Cricket). A batter is out LBW when the ball – which did not pitch outside leg stump and did not hit the bat first – hits their body in line with the stumps and would have gone on to hit the wickets. On TV, the DRS (Decision Review System) checks these three things with ball tracking: pitching, impact and wickets.

What does LBW mean?

LBW stands for Leg Before Wicket. It is one of the ways a batter can be dismissed, and it is written in Law 36 of the Laws of Cricket, which are kept by the Marylebone Cricket Club (MCC). Despite the name, it is not only the leg: the ball can hit any part of the batter’s body or equipment except the bat, or the hand holding the bat.

The idea is simple: a batter must not stop the ball from hitting the stumps with their body. The details are what make LBW the most argued-about decision in cricket – on TV and in every gully.

The LBW rules, step by step

For a batter to be given out LBW, all of these must be true:

  1. The fielding side appeals. No appeal, no LBW (“Howzat?”).
  2. It is a legal delivery – not a no-ball.
  3. The ball did not pitch outside leg stump. Pitching in line or outside off stump is fine. Pitching outside leg is always not out, even if it would have hit the stumps.
  4. The ball did not hit the bat (or the glove holding it) first. Bat first means not out.
  5. The impact was in line with the stumps – or outside off stump only if the batter made no genuine attempt to play the ball with the bat.
  6. The ball would have gone on to hit the wickets. The umpire imagines the path the ball would have taken after hitting the pad.

What is DRS in cricket?

DRS full form: Decision Review System. It lets a team challenge an on-field umpire’s decision, which is then checked by the third umpire using replays and technology. DRS was introduced in international cricket in 2009. For LBWs, the key technology is ball tracking (Hawk-Eye is the best-known system), which draws the ball’s path and predicts where it would have gone.

In Pakistan, note: “DRS” is also the name of PTA’s mobile Device Registration System. That is unrelated – in cricket, DRS always means the Decision Review System.

How DRS ball tracking decides an LBW

Ball tracking answers the LBW law as three questions, shown as three boxes on screen:

  1. Pitching – in line, outside off, or outside leg? Decided by where the centre of the ball lands.
  2. Impact – where did the ball hit the batter: in line, outside off, or outside leg?
  3. Wickets – would the ball have hit the stumps (“hitting”), missed them (“missing”), or is it too close to call (“umpire’s call”)?

If all three boxes go the bowler’s way – often called “three reds” – the batter is out. If any box goes the batter’s way, it is not out.

What does umpire’s call mean?

Umpire’s call means the review is too close to overturn, so the on-field umpire’s original decision stands. It is not a decision on its own: if the umpire said out, it stays out; if they said not out, it stays not out.

  • Wickets: to overturn a decision, at least half of the ball must be predicted to hit the wicket zone. If less than half of the ball is in the zone, it is umpire’s call. Since 2021, the zone goes up to the top of the bails.
  • Impact: the same idea applies – if the impact is only partly in line, it is umpire’s call.
  • Pitching: there is no umpire’s call for pitching. The ball either pitched outside leg or it didn’t.

Umpire’s call exists because ball tracking is a prediction with a small margin of error, so very marginal calls go back to the umpire.

LBW in gully and tape ball cricket

In gully cricket, LBW is often switched off by house rules – there is no neutral umpire, and nobody agrees whether it was going on to hit the crate. If your group does play LBW, agree the rules above before the match.

Frequently asked questions

What is the full form of LBW?

LBW stands for Leg Before Wicket. It is Law 36 of the MCC Laws of Cricket.

What is the full form of DRS in cricket?

DRS stands for Decision Review System. It lets teams challenge an umpire’s decision, which the third umpire checks using replays and ball tracking.

What are the LBW rules in cricket?

A batter is out LBW if the fielding side appeals, the ball did not pitch outside leg stump, did not hit the bat first, hit the batter in line with the stumps (or outside off with no shot offered), and would have gone on to hit the wickets.

What does umpire’s call mean in DRS?

Umpire’s call means the ball-tracking result is too close to overturn – for example, less than half the ball hitting the wicket zone – so the on-field umpire’s original decision stands.

Can you be out LBW if the ball pitches outside leg stump?

No. If the ball pitches outside leg stump, the batter is not out LBW, even if it would have hit the stumps.

Can you be out LBW if the ball hits the bat first?

No. If the ball hits the bat (or the glove holding the bat) before the pad, it is not out LBW.
