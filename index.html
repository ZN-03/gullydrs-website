What is GullyDRS?

GullyDRS is a cricket DRS (decision review system) app for street, nets and ground cricket. You put one phone behind the bowler and record the delivery. GullyDRS tracks the ball and tells you where it pitched, where it hit the batter, and whether it was going on to hit the stumps – out, not out or umpire’s call. It works offline: videos are processed on the phone and never uploaded. It is made for cricket wherever it’s played – a gully in Lahore, a lane in Mumbai, a backyard in Sydney or a club net in London.

How it works

Three steps. One phone.

No sensors, no extra cameras, no laptop. If you can record a video, you can review an LBW.

  1. Set up the phone

    On a tripod or held still behind the bowler, in line with the stumps.

  2. Record the delivery

    Start before the run-up ends, stop after the ball reaches the batter.

  3. Get the decision

    See the ball’s path, where it pitched, where it hit, and whether it was going on to the stumps.

The decisions

Every call, in the colours you know.

Just like the review on TV, GullyDRS ends with a clear answer – and admits when it can’t give one.

Out

Hitting the stumps

Pitched in line, hit in line, and the ball was going on to hit the wickets.

Not out

Missing

Pitched outside leg, hit outside the line, missing the stumps – or bat before pad.

Umpire’s call

Too close to call

Only clipping the edge of the zone. Too close to overturn – the on-field call stays.

Inconclusive

Can’t tell? It says so.

If the ball can’t be tracked reliably, GullyDRS won’t guess. The on-field decision stands.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

What is GullyDRS?

GullyDRS is a cricket decision review system (DRS) app for gully (street), nets and ground cricket. With one phone behind the bowler, it tracks the ball and shows where it pitched, where it hit and whether it was going on to hit the stumps – like ball tracking on TV.

Does GullyDRS need the internet?

No. GullyDRS processes videos on your phone and works offline. Nothing is uploaded.

Does it work with a tape ball or tennis ball?

Yes. GullyDRS is built for street cricket with a tape ball or tennis ball – even with a crate for stumps. It also works in nets, box cricket and on turf or dirt pitches.

Where should I put the phone?

Behind the bowler, in line with the stumps, on a tripod or held still. Start recording before the run-up ends and stop after the ball reaches the batter.

A fixed camera in the same net every week gives the most accurate results. On a ground, mark the creases once for full precision.

What happens if GullyDRS can’t track the ball?

It shows “inconclusive” instead of guessing, and the on-field decision stands.

What if the ball hits the bat before the pad?

When the ball clearly hits the bat before the pad, GullyDRS detects it and gives not out.

Which phones does GullyDRS work on?

Is GullyDRS free?

GullyDRS has a free plan with 20 reviews a month, which will show ads after launch. A Pro subscription, sold through Google Play, gives unlimited reviews with no ads.

What is the full form of DRS in cricket?

DRS stands for Decision Review System. In cricket it lets a team challenge an umpire’s decision, which is checked with replays and ball tracking. GullyDRS brings DRS-style ball tracking for LBWs to everyday cricket. Read our LBW & DRS guide.

What does umpire’s call mean?

Umpire’s call means the ball-tracking result is too close to overturn – for example, less than half the ball hitting the stumps zone – so the on-field umpire’s original decision stands.

Is GullyDRS related to PTA DRS in Pakistan?

No. GullyDRS is a cricket app. Here DRS means Decision Review System, not the PTA mobile Device Registration System (DIRBS).

Which countries can use GullyDRS?

GullyDRS is made for cricket wherever it is played – from Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and Afghanistan to England, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, the West Indies, the UAE and the USA. Can cricket clubs and academies use GullyDRS?

Yes. With a fixed camera in the same net, GullyDRS gives its most accurate results – ideal for clubs, academies, schools and coaches. It reviews LBWs and, in testing, adds bowling analytics such as speed, pitch maps and line and length. Videos stay on the device. See GullyDRS for academies.
